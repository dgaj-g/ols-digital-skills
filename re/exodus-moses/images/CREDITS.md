## 1. KEY TERMS — establishing image (Israelites in Egypt)

**File:** `scene1-keyterms-pharaoh-and-the-midwives.jpg` / `-1100.jpg`
- **Commons title:** File:Tissot Pharaoh and the Midwives.jpg
- **Artist:** James Jacques Joseph Tissot (French, 1836–1902)
- **Date:** c. 1896–1902
- **Collection:** The Jewish Museum, New York (gouache on board), from the "Old Testament" watercolour series
- **Licence:** Public domain (Commons tag; artist died 1902, well over 100 years ago)
- **Commons URL:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tissot_Pharaoh_and_the_Midwives.jpg
- **Source size:** 1920x2326 (1902 KB) — **Web copy:** 1100x1333 (356 KB)
- **What it shows:** Exodus 1:15–21. Pharaoh, enthroned on a raised dais under a large feathered fan-canopy, sits upper-centre-right looking down; two cloaked Hebrew midwives kneel in supplication at lower-left; an armed guard stands lower-right foreground. Portrait orientation.
- **Crop notes:** Pharaoh's throne/face sits in the upper-middle third; the midwives are lower-left. A 16:9 banner crop should keep the middle band (throne + midwives), losing some sky/river top and guard's legs at the bottom. A square crop should centre on Pharaoh's throne — strong single focal point, good centre-of-crop subject.
- **Why chosen:** Not literally in the task's example list, but a genuine high-resolution PD Tissot scene that opens the Exodus story (Pharaoh's oppression of the Israelites) — much better resolution than the two named examples below.
- **Alternate considered:** File:Tissot Moses and Aaron Speak to the People.jpg (PD, matches the brief's own suggested title exactly) — REJECTED as primary only because the Commons original is just 445x377px, too small to enlarge cleanly to a banner. Still available at `_research/t1.json` metadata if a smaller use is wanted; landscape orientation, Moses/Aaron left, crowd right.

## 2. Moses laid in the basket among the reeds

**File:** `scene2-moses-basket-laid-amid-the-flags.jpg` / `-1100.jpg`
- **Commons title:** File:Tissot Moses Laid Amid the Flags.jpg
- **Artist:** James Jacques Joseph Tissot (French, 1836–1902)
- **Date:** c. 1896–1902
- **Collection:** The Jewish Museum, New York (gouache on board, 25.2 x 12.4 cm)
- **Licence:** Public domain
- **Commons URL:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tissot_Moses_Laid_Amid_the_Flags.jpg
- **Source size:** 1497x3000 (1346 KB) — **Web copy:** 1100x2204 (500 KB)
- **What it shows:** Exodus 2:3. Moses' mother, bent forward, lays the baby in a basket at the water's edge. Very tall portrait composition: the top ~55% is tall reeds and a pale moon/sun, the mother and basket occupy only the bottom ~40% of the frame, centred horizontally.
- **Crop notes:** IMPORTANT — because the figures sit low in a very tall image, a 16:9 banner crop MUST be taken from the bottom third, not the vertical centre, or the mother and baby will be cropped out entirely. A square crop should also anchor to the bottom, roughly centred on the basket. Good, clear subject once correctly anchored.
- **Alternates considered (not downloaded, both PD, both high-res):** File:Tissot Pharaoh's Daughter Has Moses Brought to Her.jpg (1661x3000, portrait) and File:Tissot Pharaoh's Daughter Receives the Mother of Moses.jpg (3000x2409, landscape — easier 16:9 crop if a wider format is wanted later).

## 3. Moses and the burning bush

**File:** `scene3-burning-bush-bourdon.jpg` / `-1100.jpg`
- **Commons title:** File:Bourdon Sebastien-Moses and the Burning Bush.jpg
- **Artist:** Sébastien Bourdon (French, 1616–1671)
- **Date:** mid-17th century (painted before 1671)
- **Licence:** Public domain (artist died 1671)
- **Commons URL:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bourdon_Sebastien-Moses_and_the_Burning_Bush.jpg
- **Source size:** 497x640 (116 KB) — **Web copy (upscaled):** 1100x1416 (211 KB)
- **What it shows:** Moses kneels lower-left, shielding his face; God/an angel leans out of the flaming bush at centre-right, flames filling the lower-right two-thirds of the frame. Portrait orientation, clear single focal point.
- **Crop notes:** Subject fills almost the whole frame — good centre-of-crop candidate for both banner and square. Flames + figures read instantly as "burning bush" even at small size.
- **No suitable PD Tissot version exists for this scene** — Tissot's Old Testament series does include a burning-bush watercolour ("Moïse et le buisson ardent" / "Moïse adore le buisson ardent"), but the only Commons upload is via the Phillip Medhurst Collection and is licensed **CC BY-SA 4.0**, which the brief excludes. Gustave Doré's 1866 Bible engravings also do not include a burning-bush plate. Bourdon is the strongest genuine pre-1900 PD fallback found.
- **Judgement call — resolution:** the only Commons copy of this painting is a modest 497x640px scan, so the 1100px web copy is a ~2.2x upscale and will look slightly soft, not crisp, at full banner width. An alternative was tested and rejected: **File:Landscape with Moses and the Burning Bush MET DT10296.jpg** (Domenichino, 1610–1616, Met Museum, explicit **CC0**, 2808x3722 — kept in `alternates/`) is much higher resolution and fully PD/CC0, but Moses and the bush are tiny figures in the bottom-left corner of a large classical landscape; at banner/square crop sizes they would barely read as "burning bush." Recommend Bourdon for legibility; swap to the Domenichino only if a hard 1400px+ requirement outweighs clarity.

## 4. The first Passover — blood on the doorposts

**File:** `scene4-passover-signs-on-the-door.jpg` / `-1100.jpg`
- **Commons title:** File:Tissot The Signs on the Door.jpg
- **Artist:** James Jacques Joseph Tissot (French, 1836–1902) "or follower" (per Jewish Museum catalogue note — still Commons-tagged Public domain, same PD collection)
- **Date:** c. 1896–1902
- **Collection:** The Jewish Museum, New York (gouache on board, 23.8 x 11.5 cm)
- **Licence:** Public domain
- **Commons URL:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tissot_The_Signs_on_the_Door.jpg
- **Source size:** 1478x3000 (2481 KB) — **Web copy:** 1100x2233 (471 KB)
- **What it shows:** Exodus 12:7. A man in a red robe, seen from behind/side, reaches up to paint the blood sign on the door lintel with a brush, holding a small clay jug of blood in his other hand. Very tall portrait; figure runs almost the full height of the frame, centre-left.
- **Crop notes:** Strong vertical subject. A 16:9 banner crop should centre on the torso/reaching-arm area (roughly the middle third) to keep both the hand-on-lintel action and the robe; a square mobile crop should centre on the torso/waist where the red robe and blood-jug are — clear, unambiguous subject either way.
- **Alternate considered (not downloaded):** File:Tissot Passover.jpg (PD, 397x620, portrait) — smaller and less dynamic composition (a seated/standing figure), kept as a fallback name only.

## 5. The plagues — the final plague (death of the firstborn)

**File:** `scene5-plagues-firstborn-slain-dore.jpg` / `-1100.jpg`
- **Commons title:** File:035.The Firstborn of the Egyptians Are Slain.jpg
- **Artist:** Gustave Doré (French, 1832–1883), engraved by H. Pisan
- **Date:** 1866 ("Doré's English Bible")
- **Licence:** Public domain
- **Commons URL:** https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:035.The_Firstborn_of_the_Egyptians_Are_Slain.jpg
- **Source size:** 1920x2418 (2326 KB) — **Web copy:** 1100x1385 (562 KB)
- **What it shows:** Exodus 12:29. Black-and-white engraving. The winged Angel of Death, sword in hand, strides away centre-right; grieving women and a dead child are grouped lower-left and right; a family group mourns upper-left. Portrait orientation, dramatic chiaroscuro.
- **Crop notes:** The angel figure (centre-right, upright, high contrast against a pale doorway) is the strongest single focal point — good centre-of-crop subject for both banner and square. Content is sombre (mourning figures, a dead child) — worth a quick teacher look before use with younger pupils, though it is exactly the scene the brief asked for ("the final plague is the priority").
- **Judgement call — Tissot vs Doré:** Tissot's matching PD scene, File:Tissot Pharaoh and His Dead Son.jpg (kept in `alternates/tissot-deadson-check.jpg`), shows Pharaoh gesturing toward his son's body on a bier with Moses and Aaron at left — a clear, less harrowing composition — but the only Commons copy is 445x277px, too small to enlarge to a usable banner. Given the brief's own emphasis on final image quality (banner + square crop), Doré's high-resolution engraving was chosen as primary; Tissot's small version is the closer style-match to the other four images if a teacher prefers strict consistency over resolution.
