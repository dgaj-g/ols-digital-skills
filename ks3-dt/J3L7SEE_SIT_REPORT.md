# Sit report · J3 Lesson 7 "Teach It To See" · the built lesson · 13 Nov 2026

One robot pupil walked the built lesson, served, in the frame the platform really gives it (the window less 141 px), at 1280×800, 1366×768, 1440×900 and 1024×768, once with a neighbour drawing and once with the stranger's drawings. A robot hand is not a child's hand: the recogniser has still not met a real pupil. Compared against the prototype's report (L7_J3_REDESIGN/SIT_REPORT_SEE.md).

## What a pupil sees, in order
1. **Do now** (the platform's own screen). Five questions from earlier lessons.
2. **Draw anything.** A white box before she reads a thing. "No idea. It knows nothing yet. Today you teach it."
3. **Part 1 · Teach it.** Five suns, five fish, then test drawings with a bar for how sure it is.
4. **Part 2 · Fool it.** Her neighbour draws six, or "Nobody is beside me" and a stranger's six draw themselves. It gets some wrong; she teaches it those and tests again.
5. **Part 3 · Sure and wrong.** She draws a cat; it is sure and wrong. All 17 test drawings laid out by how sure it was; she picks the line under which a person checks.
6. **Part 4 · Your card.** Name, weak spot, keep. The main lesson is done here.
7. **More to try.** Five activities, any order, each one refusable.
8. **End questions.** Five questions, three "how did it go" lines, Finish. The page stays open; "Back to where I was" returns her.

## Measured
- 79 screens (stranger) and 81 (neighbour) at every size; the prototype walked 82 to 84 (it counted the Do now, which is now the platform's screen). Page errors 0. Stuck: never.
- **Fit.** The prototype was drawn for a taller frame than the platform gives. At 1366×768 and 1024×768 the frame is 627 px tall, and the buttons under the drawing box were cut off. The built page now sizes the drawing box to the room (260 to 428 px) and tightens short screens. Every screen at all four sizes: nothing cut off, no scroll, every word inside its button.
- Job line, the seven parts with the one she is in, and the green "Stuck? Show me" on every screen. No screen over 25 words.
- Help pressed on every screen of every walk: steps for that screen, a ring on the thing to use, never on something hidden, nothing covered. A grey example draws itself on every empty box. Idle: the button pulses at 20 seconds, the thing to use is ringed at 40.
- "Go to the end questions" is on every screen of every extra, and in the main lesson late in the hour. Pressed in the middle of the cat drawing: "Back to where I was" returns the same screen with the drawing still there, from the questions and from the finished screen.
- **Saving.** Her whole lesson save is 6,457 characters of the 8,000 allowed. With this computer's own copy wiped (as on another computer), reopening says welcome back and restores her 8 suns and 8 fish, her card and her finished extra. Finish records the lesson once.
- **Forced waiting.** With drawings made instantly, the whole main lesson takes about 15 seconds: nothing makes her wait. The only clock is the speed round's 90 seconds. Every other minute is her own drawing and reading.

## Where she would stall (unchanged from the prototype unless said)
1. **Drawing with a mouse** on the first sun. "Rough is fine" is step 1 of the help.
2. **Swap seats** in Part 2. "Nobody is beside me" is the way round it.
3. **The line** in Part 3: 17 small drawings and five numbers. Any number can be pressed and changed.
4. **Half and half** (extra). Hard on purpose; "Back to the activities" is always there.
5. **A real hand.** Very different suns each time could score 3 of 5 in Part 1. Not yet seen with a pupil.
6. **New: the smaller drawing box** on 768-high screens (about 300 px, not 428). The robot's drawings were still recognised at that size at every width; a child's fine detail has less room.

## The hour
Main lesson about 34 minutes (do now 4, draw anything 2, teach 8, fool 8, sure 7, card 5), as the prototype. Extras 3 to 6 minutes each. End questions 8. A slow pupil skips every extra and still finishes.
